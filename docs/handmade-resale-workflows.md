## Shared model and additive migration proposal

Reuse products as sellable stock identities, ingredients as input stock identities, recipes as food recipes or explicitly marked handmade designs, batches as production records, and the existing ledger/journals/purchase actions. No second stock, purchasing, or accounting system is introduced.

- Existing `ingredients` rows retain their ID, quantity, cost, units and type. New craft material rows explicitly use `usedAs=material`; existing supplies are not converted.
- Existing products retain all saved JSON and recipe/category/tax links. New optional product stock quantity and cost columns start at zero; they are only exposed for explicitly created handmade/resale products. No legacy quantity is guessed or copied.
- New products explicitly choose `workflow=food|handmade|resale`, independently of category Food/Non-food classification. Legacy recipe-linked records continue their existing food behavior without a data rewrite; unlinked records remain unclassified and need review. Existing stock cannot be reclassified through a metadata save.
- Each variant is a separate product with its own SKU, optional variant-group name and size/color description, cost and stock. There is no shared pool counted again at the parent level.
- The ledger gains an optional product foreign key; exactly one ingredient or product is referenced. Both references remain composite tenant keys. Existing ingredient references and rows are preserved.
- Recipes explicitly marked handmade are designs, with material/packaging lines and configured labor/overhead. Food-only ingredient/allergen editors remain on food recipes.

Recovery uses forward-only corrective migrations and reversal/adjustment records. Applied migrations and historical movements/runs/sales must never be edited. Rolling back application code can leave the additive columns in place, but must not resume old sales code for new tracked products: block writes or deploy a forward fix first. Keep a database branch/backup before publication; export records for review. No down migration deletes new stock or history.

## Costing policy, documented before implementation

Existing input receipts already use moving weighted-average stock cost while pricing estimates can use latest purchase cost. New resale and handmade finished stock use moving weighted-average cost per sellable item. A resale receipt explicitly multiplies purchase quantity by owner-entered items per purchase unit; neither case size nor freight/tax capitalization is inferred. Enter the total inventory acquisition cost deliberately, including only costs the owner has chosen to capitalize. Receiving creates a purchase journal in the existing inventory asset/Cash convention; it does not automatically mark an uploaded invoice or order paid/received.

Handmade actual usage includes explicitly entered material waste. Completed quantity means good sellable items; finished waste is separately recorded and receives no stock. All consumed input cost and configured labor/overhead are allocated across good output. Configured labor and overhead are cost allocations, not claims that payroll or cash was paid: the production journal transfers consumed input value and credits Production cost allocation for the configured non-stock portion. A production snapshot records design, rates, quantities, waste, input costs and output basis. Estimates are distinguished from stock cost. No change revalues historical transactions.

Sales consume finished stock at its current weighted-average cost and preserve that basis on the existing sale record. A full refund requires an explicit return-to-stock choice; returned goods re-enter at the original sale basis and blend with current stock. A refund without a physical return does not add stock. Adjustments use current average basis with a required reason. Food stock/cost behavior is preserved.

Reconciliation of configured labor/overhead allocations against actual payroll or operating expenses remains manual. Displayed unit profit means selling revenue excluding configured tax minus the stated unit cost, before payment fees and other business expenses. Margin means that profit divided by selling revenue excluding tax. These displays do not represent final business net profit.

## Subscription audit

The public launch pricing file is a proposal and is not reliable evidence of a customer's paid entitlement. Actual managed workspace billing is enforced by `workspace_billing` and `registration-usage`. Free currently allows 5 active recipes, 5 active products, 10 tracked input items, stock receipts/adjustments and individual covered batches; sales/refunds and advanced receipt/label/AI workflows are blocked. Handmade designs count as recipes, variants count as products, and materials count as tracked inputs under those existing limits. New flows reuse the same actions and quota checks. No billing, plan assignment, price or tier access is changed. Unmanaged pre-launch workspaces retain existing role-based access. A particular owner's subscription cannot be verified without an authenticated owner session; Netlify hosting Pro is not an app subscription.

## Exact migration mapping and recovery

The only new migration is `20261109010000_add_trade_stock`. Every previously applied migration remains unchanged. The platform applies the new migration during deployment; it was exercised only in disposable test storage during this session.

Publication validation initially found an extra unapplied generated migration, `20261103015630_handy_thunderbird`, whose timestamp preceded the latest applied migration. It duplicated the stock schema because the original custom stock migration lacked a Drizzle snapshot. The pending files were reset and the stock changes regenerated as a single `20261109010000_add_trade_stock` migration with its snapshot, retaining the deferred ledger-balance function and triggers. Applied migration files were verified byte-for-byte unchanged. A second generation reported no schema changes, and migration status confirmed 15 applied, one pending, zero missing and zero out-of-order migrations. The repair reran the workflow, inventory-count, category-tax and label-recovery database suites: 20 tests passed and one was skipped. No production build or deployment was run during the repair.

Ambiguous records require owner review outside this migration. Until reviewed, create a separate explicitly classified product/design and receive or produce new stock through supported movements; do not copy a balance or convert legacy stock silently. No legacy-data reclassification tool was added.

Recovery procedure: retain a database branch/backup before publication, review/export affected stock records, and apply forward corrections or reversing/adjustment transactions. No customer backup or production database mutation was performed in this session. An application rollback must first stop writes for the new tracked products or include their stock logic; old sales code cannot safely operate on these new products. Additive columns may remain in place. Never remove applied migrations or delete movement history as rollback.

## Tests actually performed

The complete Vitest suite passed **785 tests**, with **15 skipped**, across 93 passing files. The skipped tests were not claimed as verified. `product-workflow-database.test.ts` loaded all migrations into disposable PGlite storage and called the actual workspace handler through transactions using a non-superuser database role; it did not mutate customer records.

The handmade acceptance received 10 m of fabric at $4/m, saved/reopened a design and blanket product, consumed 2 m plus 0.5 m explicit waste, recorded 60 minutes at $12/hour and $4 other cost, and added two good blankets at $13 each. Selling one left one blanket and 7.5 m of material. A later labor-rate change left the completed run's $26 basis intact. Retrying completion/sale did not duplicate movements.

The resale acceptance received two cases of 12 items for $72, giving 24 items at $3 each. Selling three left 21. A later receipt added three items for $18; the original sale's full physical return restored three items at the original $3 basis, producing 27 items valued at $90. A two-item breakage adjustment left 25. Receipt/return/adjustment retries did not repeat stock, and a refund without restock preserved the remaining quantity.

All **25 Chromium browser checks passed** using deterministic synthetic workspace responses, separate from the actual-handler database tests. They included every canonical section/tab, legacy links, responsive overflow, visible active tabs, uploaded photos, product/design save-reopen, receipt and production editor payloads, browser history, dirty drafts, packaging and label/update regressions. A final focused history/editor check also covered opening the food editor from the Handmade tab. Desktop and phone previews are available in [1440-pixel](previews/handmade-resale-1440.webp) and [390-pixel](previews/handmade-resale-390.webp) images; these contain synthetic records.

TypeScript and changed-source formatting/whitespace checks were run. Focused lint passed for the new workflow, navigation and acceptance files; a full-repository lint pass was not claimed.
