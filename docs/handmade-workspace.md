# Handmade production and costing

Handmade is a main navigation section with Overview, Materials & Supplies, Products & Bills of Materials, Production, and Finished Inventory. It uses the existing catalog product IDs and stock ledger. It does not create food recipes for new handmade products. Craft materials retain their existing inventory records, are filtered separately from food ingredients, and shared packaging is referenced directly rather than copied.

## Inspection and preservation

The previous handmade workflow stored craft materials in the tenant-scoped ingredients table with `usedAs: material`, handmade designs in recipes with `workflow: handmade`, and finished goods in the shared products table. Existing production, ledger, journal, sale, refund, fabric, category tax and photo workflows were inspected before implementation. The read-only preview inventory query returned zero handmade products, handmade designs and materials in its accessible database scope. No real inventory or orders were changed for testing.

The safe reusable parts were server-verified workspace ownership, forced database tenant policies, serialized workspace transactions, immutable ledger/usage/completed-run records, weighted-average inventory, and historical sale/refund cost snapshots. Requiring a recipe-shaped design for every new handmade product and exposing a store-page import during product creation were replaced.

New BOMs live in the product's existing extensible database document. No new SQL columns or tables were needed; applied migrations were left untouched. Legacy designs, products and materials were not renamed, deleted, duplicated or classified by their names. Legacy designs are listed in Handmade, and linked products can adopt a product-owned BOM explicitly by saving their editor. Their original design link, stock, cost, photos, description, prices, completed runs and sales remain intact. Orphan designs can explicitly seed a new catalog product. No historical BOM versions, production runs or unknown costs are manufactured.

## Daily workflow

1. Add a material with its stock unit, category, supplier, SKU, notes and reorder threshold. Receive actual purchases with purchase quantity, purchase unit, explicitly confirmed usable stock units per purchase unit, actual total acquisition cost, date and optional supplier reference. Each receipt appears in the immutable stock/cost history. Existing fabric opens the original fabric editor and receipt workflow to preserve width and remnant rules.
2. Create a handmade catalog product, upload/select its photo, set category/tax treatment, description, SKU, selling price and active/archive state. For variants, create separate products with a common variant group and independent BOMs, SKUs and finished balances.
3. Enter BOM output, material and optional shared-packaging lines, material scrap allowance, direct labor minutes and hourly rate, production overhead per run, and instructions. Known standard unit relationships are used for BOM quantities; unsupported relationships require existing verified material-specific conversions. Sheets are not treated as pieces, and no density is guessed. The purchase conversion records the actual usable contents explicitly.
4. Start a run from a product, enter planned finished units and review requirements, missing costs and shortages. Confirming a plan snapshots the BOM/version, component units, labor rate and allocations. Planning moves no stock.
5. Record actual component consumption (including packaging), additional material scrap, actual labor minutes, actual sellable output, finished scrap and its reason. Completion takes current stock acquisition costs, consumes actual inputs and receives sellable output atomically. It retains the pinned BOM, instructions, usage rows, cost snapshot and balanced journal. Stable run IDs plus serialized transactions prevent repeated completion from posting twice.
6. Record a sale against the same catalog product through existing Sales. Finished stock is reduced and its sale-time average cost is recorded once as COGS. A full refund with physical restock returns units at the original sale cost and reverses that COGS. A financial-only refund leaves stock and COGS unchanged. Duplicate refund requests are rejected.

## Cost basis

BOM costs are planning estimates using current weighted-average stock unit costs. Expected scrap allowance increases planned component use, including packaging. The displayed breakdown separates materials, packaging, direct labor and allocated production overhead. Total cost divided by BOM output gives estimated cost per sellable unit. Enter zero explicitly for excluded labor or overhead; the editor identifies those exclusions. Gross profit uses tax-exclusive selling revenue minus estimated unit cost; margin divides that profit by tax-exclusive revenue, not by cost. Selling expenses are excluded and remain separate.

Recorded production cost uses actual stock consumption at completion, actual labor minutes at the pinned rate and the planned run's pinned overhead allocation. Actual sellable output is the denominator. Completed production and sale costs never recalculate from later BOM edits or supplier purchases.

Purchases debit Inventory asset and credit Cash, consistent with the existing acquisition journal convention. Production transfers material inventory into finished inventory and credits configured labor/overhead to Production cost allocation. Neither purchases nor completed production become sales COGS immediately. Finished goods use perpetual weighted-average valuation. A sale debits Finished goods COGS and credits Inventory asset. This retains the application's existing ledger method; it does not configure or change a business's tax accounting election. Configured labor/overhead allocation does not itself record payroll or an overhead bill.

## Corrections and limits

Owner/Manager whole-run reversal appends compensating material and finished-stock movements and a reversing journal. The completed run and original journal remain immutable. Reversal removes original output value and restores original material costs into current weighted-average stock. It is idempotent. It requires available finished output and blocks when downstream negative finished-stock movements occurred after completion; automatic downstream disassembly is not implemented. Create a new run for corrected production. Legacy completed runs without the product-production snapshot retain their original immutable history and are not automatically reversed.

Runs currently require at least one sellable unit and whole finished-unit counts; partial reversals, lot/FIFO costing, serial tracking, multi-stage work in progress, actual hourly-rate/overhead overrides, and partial customer returns are not implemented. Full-loss production can be recorded using the existing reasoned stock-adjustment workflow. Fabric keeps its existing specialized inventory rules. Existing receipts and historical values with unknown cost require explicit review; zero is never invented as a substitute. A new material receipt with unknown positive opening stock, production into unknown positive finished inventory, and sales of unknown-cost finished stock are blocked.

Purchase conversions are receipt-specific. Saved verified material conversions are available to BOMs through the existing unit-conversion workflow; the new BOM editor does not add a density-estimation tool. Legacy records remain available for review rather than receiving guessed historical costs. Payment provider settings, subscriptions and live checkout were not changed.

## Validation

Automated tests used isolated PGlite fixture businesses and React component fixtures, with no demonstration writes to actual inventory or orders. Coverage included multiple materials, yard/foot conversion and explicit purchase contents, multiple-unit production, shared packaging, scrap/lower output, missing costs, atomic rollback on shortages, differing variant BOMs, later supplier/BOM changes, duplicate completion/receipts, reversal and duplicate reversal, sale/physical return and financial-only refund, legacy record preservation, tenant isolation and staff/accountant restrictions. TypeScript checking and catalog/editor/production/Stripe regression tests were also run. No build or local development server was run.
